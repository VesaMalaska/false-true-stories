# False True Stories — World Seed: Old Herring’s Wake

This document contains the canonical starting truth for this Story Pack.

It defines what must be true when a new Story Run begins, what should remain unknown, and where the narrator has creative freedom.

It does not contain events or consequences belonging to any particular Story Run.

## Runtime readiness

**Ready to play** — the World Seed has crossed the playability threshold.

## Core premise

Herman “Herring” Herrold is an aging seaman who owns and lives aboard **M/S CBC**, a small, rusty ship moored in a quiet coastal harbour. He occasionally hosts a clandestine poker club aboard her.

One night, Herring receives an impossible message—the first apparent intrusion of something inexplicable into his previously ordinary experience. The message creates an urgent reason to consider an open-sea voyage, but Herring cannot safely take CBC into open waters alone.

Before he can follow where the message leads, he must find one or two companions among the harbour regulars. Doing so means gambling his life on familiar people whose true motives, capabilities, and loyalties he does not know.

## Protagonist or listener position

- **The protagonist:** Herman Herrold, known along the waterfront as “Herring” or “Old Herring.” He is an experienced and capable seaman who has spent the last few years close to port, making only occasional short coastal trips he can manage single-handedly.
- **The ship:** Herring’s vessel is officially named **M/S CBC** and is ordinarily called **CBC**. The name is canonical. The ship must not be renamed.
- **Listener position:** The listener follows Herring closely and influences his critical decisions—evaluating potential crew, choosing whom to trust, deciding what to reveal, managing crises aboard CBC, and navigating the unknown.
- **Perspective:** Close third-person storytelling focused on Herring’s senses, practical seamanship, instincts, and personal judgment. If Herring dies under the strict conditions defined in Tone and danger, the perspective shifts to the already-established successor protagonist.

The letters **CBC** must not be expanded, reinterpreted, or treated as an abbreviation whose meaning requires explanation. No meaning behind those letters belongs to the narrator, characters, listener, or story.

## Initial relationships

- **The harbour community:** Herring is a familiar and established figure in the local port. Through long years around the harbour, he knows nearly every regular face encountered along the piers, in the harbour pub, around the poker table, and in the darker nearby streets.
- **The poker circle:** A rotating group of locals and passing sailors participates in Herring’s secret after-hours poker club aboard CBC. Herring knows how many of them behave around cards, but that does not tell him how they would behave during a dangerous and unbelievable voyage.
- **Potential companions:** No crew members are predetermined. Anyone Herring approaches is an open question. Familiarity does not equal reliability, and reputation does not necessarily reveal a person’s deeper motives or hidden capabilities.

## Reality and boundaries

- **The world:** A grounded, slightly timeless modern maritime setting with fictional geography, coastal towns, islands, and maritime institutions.
- **Physics and everyday technology:** Ordinary maritime physics and recognizable technology govern everyday life. Diesel engines, lead-acid batteries, radar screens, analog compasses, radio static, mechanical wear, hull damage, and dangerous weather behave credibly. Engines fail if neglected, hulls leak if struck, and the sea demands respect.
- **The impossible element:** The message is the first apparent intrusion into Herring’s previously ordinary experience. It does not prove that reality has fractured.
- **Open explanation:** The message and any later strangeness must not initially be limited to or excluded from technological, folkloric, apparently magical, altered-reality, or other possible explanations. Any eventual truth must be earned through the discoveries of the story.
- **Absolute exclusion:** **No horror.** The story must never drift into cosmic dread, gore, visceral disgust, body horror, or hopeless torment. Danger comes from high adventure, mechanical failures, unpredictable environments, tactical hazards, difficult decisions, and conflicting human motives.

## World atmosphere

The world should have lived-in, tactile maritime realism:

- peeling hull paint;
- cold harbour wind;
- damp wool;
- grease on steel tools;
- the vibration of an idling diesel engine;
- the hum of an auxiliary generator;
- poker chips clinking inside CBC’s warm cabin;
- the smells of low tide, old timber, metal, and fuel;
- familiar voices carrying across the docks at night.

The harbour offers enclosed familiarity and imperfect safety. Beyond its lights lies the immense and lonely mystery of the open sea.

The ordinary world should feel solid enough that the impossible genuinely feels impossible when it intrudes.

## Tone and danger

- **Tone:** Classic, gripping maritime adventure with a mind-stretching speculative mystery. The story is spirited, gritty, resourceful, and driven by ingenuity, courage, human relationships, and discovery.
- **Danger level:** Difficult situations and consequential dilemmas occur frequently. Equipment may fail, weather may overwhelm, plans may collapse, supplies may be lost, and human loyalties may fracture.
- **Consequences:** Mistakes may cause practical and lasting consequences, including damage to CBC, lost supplies, injuries, missed opportunities, damaged trust, and changed relationships.
- **Mortality:** Death is possible, including Herring’s death, but it must never occur casually, prematurely, arbitrarily, or merely for shock.
- **Succession boundary:** Herring’s death would be a major transformation of the story. It may occur only after another character has already developed a strong emotional connection with the listener and has become genuinely capable of carrying the story forward as the new central protagonist.
- **No prepared succession:** The identity of any potential successor and the circumstances of such a transition must remain unknown and emerge naturally through play. Herring’s possible death is a boundary governing future storytelling—not a prepared twist, predetermined event, or plan the narrator is expected to execute.

## Opening situation

- **Place:** Aboard M/S CBC, tied securely to the harbour pier on a quiet night.
- **Circumstance:** The story begins either during the final hands of a secret poker game or in the lingering quiet immediately afterward.
- **Inciting event:** The impossible message arrives through a medium or event that defies an ordinary explanation.
- **Immediate problem:** The message creates an urgent reason for Herring to consider a voyage into open waters. CBC cannot safely make that voyage under Herring’s solo hand.
- **First pressure:** Herring must decide whom he might approach, how much he can safely reveal, and whether any familiar harbour face can be trusted with the truth—or with his life.

The runtime must begin inside this situation rather than explaining the wider world beforehand.

## Canonical starting truths

1. Herman Herrold, known as Herring or Old Herring, is an experienced aging seaman with deep roots in a fictional coastal harbour.
2. Herring owns and lives aboard a small, aging, rusty vessel officially named **M/S CBC** and ordinarily called **CBC**.
3. The name **M/S CBC** is fixed canon. The ship must not be renamed, and the letters CBC must not be expanded or assigned an in-story meaning.
4. CBC is sound enough for the short coastal work Herring performs alone, but Herring cannot safely take her on a serious open-sea voyage without one or two additional crew members.
5. Herring has had no active crew during his recent harbour-bound years.
6. Herring occasionally operates a secret poker club aboard CBC.
7. Through the poker club and his long history around the harbour, Herring knows nearly every regular face in the surrounding waterfront community.
8. Until this night, Herring has never personally encountered anything that convincingly violated ordinary physical or technological explanations.
9. An impossible message has arrived, marking the first apparent intrusion of something inexplicable into Herring’s previously ordinary experience.
10. Herring has not yet chosen or secured anyone to sail with him.

## Character knowledge and beliefs

- Herring knows the harbour, its regular faces, the practical limits of CBC, and the ordinary rules and dangers of the sea.
- Herring knows that he cannot safely undertake the implied open-sea voyage alone.
- He does not know the message’s true source, mechanism, purpose, or ultimate meaning.
- He does not know which potential companions would accept, refuse, betray, protect, manipulate, surprise, or prove themselves during the voyage.
- Familiarity and reputation may inform Herring’s judgment, but neither gives him access to another person’s private motives.
- The listener initially knows no more than Herring about the message, the deeper nature of reality, or the true character of potential companions.
- The narrator may know or develop truths that Herring and the listener do not yet know, but must preserve the distinction between world truth, character knowledge, listener knowledge, and narration.

## Intentionally unknown

The following matters are reserved for runtime discovery:

- the exact wording and immediate form of the impossible message;
- the message’s source, mechanism, purpose, and ultimate meaning;
- whether the strangeness is technological, folkloric, apparently magical, reality-altering, or something else entirely;
- the origins of Herman’s nicknames “Herring” and “Old Herring”;
- which harbour faces will offer, refuse, demand, or scheme to join the voyage;
- the personal histories, true motives, loyalties, and hidden capabilities of potential companions;
- whether any companion will prove reliable, treacherous, conflicted, or something more complicated;
- the phenomena waiting beyond the harbour lights;
- how far the strangeness surrounding the message ultimately reaches;
- whether Herring ever dies;
- the identity of any character who might one day become capable of succeeding Herring;
- the circumstances under which such a succession could occur.

These unknowns are protected creative space. They must not be answered before play merely to complete the world’s documentation.

## Narrator freedom

The runtime narrator may:

- invent the harbour town’s name, layout, customs, institutions, streets, pub, docks, and surrounding waters;
- create poker players, dockworkers, sailors, pub regulars, travellers, and potential companions with distinct voices, histories, flaws, apparent motives, and private truths;
- decide the exact wording and sensory manner in which the impossible message arrives—for example through a dead radio channel, an anomalous printout, an unexpected delivery, or another fitting event;
- develop the origin of Herring’s nickname if and when it becomes naturally relevant;
- design maritime hazards, equipment failures, weather patterns, navigation challenges, tactical problems, and human conflicts;
- create consequential off-screen events that arise naturally from established circumstances and choices;
- allow relationships, trust, suspicion, loyalty, and emotional connection to develop through interaction and crisis;
- introduce further mind-stretching phenomena without prematurely fixing the ultimate explanation;
- eventually establish answers to open mysteries when those answers have been genuinely earned by the story.

The narrator must:

- preserve all canonical starting truths and established runtime truths;
- keep CBC’s name fixed as **M/S CBC**;
- never invent, reveal, or imply an expanded meaning for the letters CBC;
- respect the strict exclusion of horror;
- respect the mortality and succession boundaries;
- avoid treating any possible successor as a predetermined replacement;
- preserve important consequences once they become true.

## Runtime handoff

This World Seed is the canonical starting point for a new Story Run.

The story runtime must:

- begin directly inside the opening situation aboard M/S CBC;
- preserve all canonical starting truths, the grounded maritime atmosphere, and the strict exclusion of horror;
- keep separate Story Runs isolated;
- keep the name M/S CBC fixed and leave the letters CBC unexplained;
- leave the ultimate nature of the impossible message open until discoveries within the story establish otherwise;
- treat prospective companions as genuine unknowns whose loyalties and capabilities are revealed through interaction and crisis;
- respect the mortality boundary, ensuring Herring’s death is never arbitrary and occurs only after another character has developed a strong emotional bond with the listener and become capable of carrying the story;
- distinguish world truth from character knowledge, listener knowledge, and narration;
- keep Herring grounded in what he can realistically know, perceive, believe, and infer;
- present choices sparingly and only at moments of genuine decision;
- preserve consequential events, discoveries, choices, relationship changes, and other state changes in the selected Story Run;
- allow the remaining unknowns to be discovered through the story.

Stop designing the world and begin the story.
