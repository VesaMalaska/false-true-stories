# False True Stories — World Seed: Old Herring’s Wake

This document contains the canonical starting truth for this Story Pack.

It defines what must be true when a new Story Run begins, what should remain unknown, and where the narrator has creative freedom.

It does not contain events or consequences belonging to any particular Story Run.

## Runtime readiness

**Ready to play** — the World Seed has crossed the playability threshold.

## Core premise

Herman “Herring” Herrold, an aging sailor who maintains a rusty ship in a quiet harbor town and hosts a clandestine poker club aboard her, receives an impossible message that marks the first apparent intrusion of something inexplicable into his previously ordinary life. To follow where the message leads, he must immediately confront a grounded, dangerous obstacle: he cannot sail the open ocean alone, and recruiting one or two companions from the harbor regulars means gambling his life on people whose true motives he does not know.

## Protagonist or listener position

- **The Protagonist:** Herman Herrold, known along the waterfront as “Herring” or “Old Herring.” He is an experienced, capable seaman who has spent the last few years staying close to port, handling only occasional short coastal runs he can manage single-handedly.
- **Listener Position:** The listener follows Herring closely, making his critical decisions—evaluating potential crew, choosing whom to trust, managing crises aboard ship, and navigating the unknown.
- **Perspective:** Close third-person storytelling focused on Herring’s senses, practical seamanship, instincts, and personal judgment. In the event of Herring’s death under the specific criteria defined in Tone and danger, the perspective shifts to the established successor.

## Initial relationships

- **The Harbor Community:** Herring is a fixture in the local port. He knows the faces, habits, and reputations of the fishermen, dockworkers, gamblers, and drifters who frequent the pier, the harbor pub, and the dark streets nearby.
- **The Poker Circle:** A rotating group of locals and passing sailors who step aboard his ship for after-hours cards. Herring knows how they play, but he does not know who they truly are when ordinary life is stripped away.
- **Potential Companions:** Anyone Herring approaches for the voyage is an open question. Familiarity does not equal reliability.

## Reality and boundaries

- **The World:** A grounded, timeless modern maritime setting with fictional geography, coastal towns, islands, and maritime authorities.
- **Physics & Everyday Technology:** Diesel smoke, salt spray, lead-acid batteries, radar screens, analog compasses, radio static, and authentic nautical physics govern everyday life. Engines fail if neglected; hulls leak if struck; weather demands respect.
- **The Impossible Element:** The impossible message is the first apparent intrusion into Herring’s previously ordinary experience. Its underlying source, nature, and explanation are completely open: they must not be canonically limited to or excluded from technology, folklore, apparently magical phenomena, altered reality, or other possibilities. Whatever unfolds must remain mind-stretching and wondrous.
- **Absolute Exclusion:** **No horror.** The story must never drift into cosmic dread, gore, visceral disgust, body horror, or hopeless torment. Danger comes from high adventure, mechanical breakdowns, unpredictable environments, tactical hazards, and conflicting human motives.

## World atmosphere

- Lived-in, tactile maritime realism: peeling hull paint, cold harbor wind, damp wool, grease on steel tools, the hum of an idling auxiliary generator, clinking poker chips in a warm cabin, and the smell of low tide and diesel.
- A strong contrast between the cozy, enclosed safety of the harbor and the immense, lonely mystery of the open sea at night.

## Tone and danger

- **Tone:** Classic, gripping maritime adventure with a mind-bending speculative mystery. Spirited, gritty, resourceful, and driven by ingenuity and courage.
- **Danger Level:** High frequency of difficult situations and dilemmas. Real peril exists: equipment can fail, weather can overwhelm, and human loyalties can fracture.
- **Consequences & Mortality:** Mistakes carry practical, durable consequences such as damage to the ship, lost supplies, strained relationships, and injuries. Death is possible, including Herring’s death, but it must never happen casually, prematurely, arbitrarily, or merely for shock. Herring’s death would represent a major story transformation. It may occur only if another character has already developed a strong emotional connection with the listener and has become capable of carrying the story forward as the new central protagonist. The identity of any potential successor and the circumstances of such a transition must remain completely unknown and emerge naturally through play.

## Opening situation

- **Place:** Aboard Herring’s weathered ship, tied securely to the harbor pier on a quiet night.
- **Circumstance:** Either during the final hands of a secret poker game or in the lingering quiet immediately following it, the impossible message arrives—the first apparent sign that reality may hold something far stranger than Herring believed.
- **Immediate Problem:** The message demands an immediate voyage into open waters, forcing Herring to decide how to secure one or two crew members without giving away more than he should or trusting the wrong soul.

## Canonical starting truths

1. Herman Herrold (“Herring”) owns and lives on an aging, rusty vessel moored in a fictional coastal port.
2. The vessel is sound enough for coastal work under Herring’s solo hand, but safely taking her into deep, open sea requires at least one or two additional crew members.
3. Herring operates a private, underground poker game aboard the ship to supplement his living and stay connected to harbor life.
4. Until tonight, Herring has never personally encountered anything that convincingly violated ordinary physical or technological explanations.
5. An impossible message has arrived, marking the first apparent intrusion into Herring’s previously ordinary experience.
6. Herring has not yet chosen or secured anyone to sail with him.

## Intentionally unknown

The following matters are reserved strictly for runtime discovery:

- The exact name painted on the ship’s transom.
- The origins of Herman’s nickname, “Herring.”
- The source, nature, and explanation of the impossible message—whether rooted in technology, folklore, apparently magical phenomena, altered reality, or something else entirely.
- Which harbor faces will offer, refuse, or demand to join the voyage.
- The true loyalty, secret motives, and hidden capabilities of whichever companions Herring chooses to bring aboard.
- The identity of any potential successor protagonist, should Herring ever die, and the events surrounding such a succession.
- The exact phenomena waiting out past the harbor lights.

## Narrator freedom

The runtime narrator is free to:

- Invent the layout, name, and specific sensory details of the harbor town, the pub, the docks, and surrounding waters.
- Create and portray poker players, dockhands, and potential crew members with distinct voices, flaws, and histories.
- Decide the immediate sensory manner in which the impossible message manifests—for example, through a dead radio channel, an anomalous printout, or an unexpected arrival—provided it feels genuinely impossible and mind-bending rather than horrifying.
- Design maritime hazards, equipment troubles, weather patterns, and navigation challenges encountered along the way.
- Develop companion characters over time, allowing mutual trust and emotional connection with the listener to form organically before any major protagonist transition could ever become possible.

## Runtime handoff

This World Seed is the canonical starting point for a new Story Run.

The story runtime must:

- begin directly inside the opening situation aboard Herring’s ship;
- preserve all canonical starting truths, the grounded maritime atmosphere, and the strict exclusion of horror;
- keep separate Story Runs isolated;
- leave the ultimate nature of the impossible message open to discovery across technological, folkloric, apparently magical, reality-bending, or other lines;
- treat prospective crew members as genuine unknowns whose loyalties and capabilities must be revealed through interaction and crisis;
- respect the mortality boundary, ensuring Herring’s death is never arbitrary and occurs only after a worthy successor has developed a strong emotional bond with the listener;
- distinguish world truth from character and listener knowledge, keeping Herring grounded in what he can realistically know and perceive;
- present choices sparingly and only at moments of genuine decision;
- preserve consequential events, discoveries, choices, and state changes in the selected Story Run;
- allow the remaining unknowns to be discovered through the story.

Stop designing the world and begin the story.
